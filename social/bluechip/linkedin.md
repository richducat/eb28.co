## Post 1

Wall Street has a term for the money regular people invest. 'Dumb money.'

I've met that money. It comes off a night shift at 7am. It comes from an invoice a plumber chased for six weeks. It comes out of a teacher's summer paycheck. There is nothing dumb about it. It's the hardest-earned money in the market.

What retail traders lack isn't intelligence. It's equipment. Institutions run desks: watchlists, journals, risk limits, kill switches, process. Retail got an app with confetti.

So we built Bluechip, a small US-equities desk a working person can run. It trades $5 fractional clips through Robinhood's official Agentic Trading API (no affiliation, we just use the front door), inside a sub-account the operator creates and controls. Paper mode first. One switch live, one switch back.

And its entire tape is public at eb28.co/fundmanager, losses included.

It will not promise anyone profits. Nothing honest can. It shows its work instead.

Dumb money just got a desk. It's in live beta. Early access: social@eb28.co, subject 'Bluechip early access.'

## Post 2

Good power tools ship with blade guards. Trading software should too.

When we designed Bluechip, EB28's US-equities desk, we borrowed more from tool design than from finance:

A torque limit: $5 fractional clips, max two buys per 15-minute cycle, eight watched names. Small by design during beta.

A guard: the desk is confined to a dedicated, isolated Robinhood Agentic sub-account the user creates. It cannot reach the rest of the account.

A dead-man switch: a global kill switch, plus paper-and-review-first before anything runs live. One switch to go live, one switch back. The operator holds it, always.

An inspection log: every decision journaled and streamed to a public dashboard at eb28.co/fundmanager, losses included.

It runs on Robinhood's official Agentic Trading API, where every order passes a broker-side review step. (No affiliation; Robinhood doesn't endorse or sponsor us.)

None of this promises anyone a profit. Trading carries real risk of loss, full stop. But safety interlocks and honest specs are what separate equipment from gadgets. We're building equipment.

## Post 3

A contractor shows you references. Real ones, including the job that ran over.

Most trading software shows you a green screenshot.

We went the other way. Our public dashboard at eb28.co/fundmanager streams every decision our Bluechip desk makes, as it makes them, losses included. Our own product page prints its negative test PnL, on purpose.

Three things happened.

The 'how much will I make' emails mostly stopped. The 'how does the kill switch work' emails started. Those are our people.

We got more careful. When your test numbers live on your homepage, you stop tuning the demo and start tuning the process.

And we sleep fine. We sell tools, discipline, and control. We have never once had to defend a promise about returns, because we have never made one.

Bluechip is in live beta: $5 fractional clips, US equities only, an isolated sub-account, and the operator holding the switch. The tape is public. Go read it before you believe a word we say.
