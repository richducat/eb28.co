## Understand what “WDO” means in the Florida report

FDACS Form 13645 defines a wood-destroying organism as arthropod or plant life that damages and can reinfest seasoned wood in a structure. The form names termites, powder post beetles, old house borers and wood-decaying fungi. That scope is broader than termites, but it does not turn the report into a general mold, indoor-air-quality or structural engineering assessment.

The distinction matters when a property owner searches by a familiar phrase. A page can use “termite inspection” in plain language and still explain when a formal WDO scope is required. It should not imply that every pest service produces a transaction-ready state form or that every visible wood condition has the same cause.

- Termites
- Powder post beetles
- Old house borers
- Wood-decaying fungi
- Not a general mold or structural report

Sources: https://forms.fdacs.gov/13645.pdf

## State the purpose before asking for a date

Tell the company whether the request supports a property sale, purchase, refinance requirement, termite renewal, visible activity, prior-treatment follow-up or a general maintenance question. If it is tied to a transaction, provide the property address, structures to include, deadline, requesting party and report recipients. Do not assume a service estimate satisfies a lender, insurer or contract requirement.

Florida Statute 482.226 requires a prescribed WDO inspection report when a licensee performs the inspection for a real-estate transaction and charges a fee or the customer requests a written report. The statute lists required information, including the date, structure, inaccessible areas, evidence, organism identity and visible damage. The exact transaction need should be confirmed with the relevant professional, not guessed from marketing copy.

- Transaction or nontransaction purpose
- Property and structures
- Deadline and recipients
- Required form or contract language

## Prepare visible access without creating damage

Move ordinary stored items away from accessible wall edges, open unlocked closets and utility areas, identify attic or crawl-space access and arrange entry to detached structures that belong in the scope. Secure pets and provide gate access through a private confirmed channel. Ask the inspector in advance how much clearance is useful rather than dismantling rooms.

Do not remove wall coverings, flooring, fixed cabinets, insulation or structural finishes. Form 13645 explicitly notes that concealed or inaccessible areas—including areas blocked by furniture, equipment, stored items and insulation—may not be covered when inspection would require removal or defacement. Preparation improves access; it does not erase the report’s limitations.

- Reasonable wall-edge clearance
- Attic and crawl-space access identified
- Detached structures available
- Pets and gates planned
- No destructive opening

Sources: https://forms.fdacs.gov/13645.pdf

## Gather the records that explain the property history

Collect prior WDO reports, termite contracts, renewal documents, treatment notices, invoices, warranties, repair records and known moisture or roof history. Keep dates and provider names visible. If the seller, buyer or property manager holds different records, decide who will send the complete packet and who should receive the new report.

Do not describe an old treatment as active protection unless the current contract and provider confirm it. Florida law requires treatment contracts to state whether they offer repair and retreatment, retreatment only, or no warranty or guarantee, and to disclose limitations. The exact document—not a remembered sales phrase—controls that understanding.

- Prior WDO reports
- Treatment and renewal contracts
- Invoices and notices
- Repair and moisture history
- Current warranty terms if any

## Read the report in three passes

First, read the structures and areas included. Second, read the visible findings: live organisms, evidence or damage and the locations described. Third, read inaccessible areas, treatment evidence and limitations. This order prevents a single checked box from overshadowing what the inspector could and could not see.

The official form states that the report is based on visible, readily accessible areas and does not guarantee the absence of WDOs or damage unless it specifically states the extent of a guarantee. It also says the report is not a structural-damage report. A structural concern should be routed to an appropriately qualified building professional, while organism identification and treatment remain in the licensed pest-control scope.

- Scope and structures
- Visible findings and locations
- Inaccessible areas
- Prior-treatment evidence
- Limitations and separate structural questions

Sources: https://forms.fdacs.gov/13645.pdf

## Ask the inspector six precise questions

Ask which license and identification credentials apply; whether Form 13645 is being issued; which structures and areas are in scope; what remains inaccessible; how photographs or diagrams are included; and what separate follow-up is recommended for treatment or structural evaluation. If a treatment contract is offered, ask for the organism, method, covered areas, renewal terms and warranty category in writing.

UF/IFAS advises homeowners to verify Florida pest-control credentials, understand the contract and expect recommendations to follow inspection. A high-quality service page can turn those questions into an appointment-preparation rail, which improves conversion by reducing uncertainty rather than by promising an outcome.

- Who is qualified to inspect?
- Does Form 13645 apply?
- What is included and inaccessible?
- How are findings documented?
- What follow-up is separate?
- What do the contract terms cover?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a WDO inspection the same as a termite inspection?

A Florida WDO report covers more than termites: the state form also names certain beetles, borers and wood-decaying fungi. However, not every general termite conversation produces a transaction-ready Form 13645. State the purpose and ask which report applies.

### What areas should be accessible for a WDO inspection?

Ask the inspector, then provide reasonable access to wall edges, closets, utility areas, attic or crawl-space entries and included detached structures. Do not remove fixed finishes or open construction. Concealed and inaccessible areas should be identified as limitations in the report.

### Does a clear WDO report guarantee there is no hidden damage?

No. Florida’s form says the inspection is based on visible, readily accessible areas and is not a structural-damage report. Concealed conditions may remain. Structural soundness questions belong with the appropriate qualified building professional.

### What prior records should I provide?

Gather previous WDO reports, treatment notices, contracts, renewal documents, invoices, repair records and known moisture history. Keep original dates and terms. Do not describe old coverage as active unless the current provider and contract confirm it.
