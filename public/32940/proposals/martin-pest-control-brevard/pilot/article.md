## Quick answer: how to prepare for pest inspection in Brevard County

Before requesting a pest inspection in Brevard County, write down the exact room or exterior area, the date and time, whether the activity repeats, any recent moisture or construction change, and whether you have a clear photo. Do not crush, sweep away or spray every sign before the appointment. Keep termite or wood-destroying-organism concerns separate from general household pest questions so the company can route the request correctly.

## Start with observations, not a diagnosis

A useful service note begins with nouns and timestamps: “three winged insects at the east living-room window at 6:40 p.m.” is more actionable than “the house has termites.” Include the floor, room, wall or exterior side; the nearest door, window, pipe or appliance; and whether the sign was moving, scattered or attached to a surface. If the activity happened more than once, list each date instead of compressing the pattern into “always.”

That distinction matters in Florida because household pests, termites and other wood-destroying organisms sit in different inspection and licensing categories. UF/IFAS advises homeowners to ask questions and select service after an inspection rather than treating a square-foot quote as a diagnosis. A factual note gives the inspector a starting point without asking a photograph to do the work of an on-site assessment.

- Exact room or exterior side
- Date and approximate time
- How many signs or insects were visible
- Whether the same pattern has repeated

## Build a photo record an inspector can follow

Take one wide photo that establishes the room or exterior elevation, one medium photo that shows the sign in relation to trim, flooring or equipment, and one close photo for detail. Keep the original files so their timestamps remain available. Rename a copy with a plain label such as “garage-south-wall-2026-07-14” and avoid filters that change color or texture.

Do not pull apart finished walls, probe structural wood or move heavy equipment for a better angle. A safe photo of a visible, accessible area is enough for the first conversation. The current Florida WDO report itself distinguishes visible, readily accessible areas from enclosed or concealed areas, which is a useful reminder that a homeowner photo set is context—not a certification that hidden areas are clear.

- Wide context photo
- Medium location photo
- Close detail photo
- Original timestamp preserved

## Separate termite signs from general pest activity

Termite concerns deserve their own line in the request. FDACS identifies multiple termite groups in Florida and stresses that correct identification matters because treatment paths differ. UF/IFAS describes mud tubes as a common subterranean-termite sign and drywood frass as gritty, six-sided pellets that can collect below small kick-out holes. Those descriptions are educational clues, not permission to self-diagnose or begin treatment.

If you see discarded wings, pellets, a tube-like trail, blistered wood or a hollow-sounding area, photograph the condition and leave it as intact as practical. Note nearby moisture, roof or plumbing work, wood-to-soil contact and the date of any prior termite service you know about. Then ask whether the request should be handled as a termite/WDO inspection rather than folded into routine household pest service.

- Photograph wings, pellets or tubes in place
- Note moisture and recent repairs
- Find prior inspection or treatment records
- Ask for the correct termite/WDO route

## Record the changes around the property

Pest activity is easier to discuss when the timeline includes changes around the home. List recent roof or plumbing leaks, landscape work, mulch added against the structure, deliveries in cardboard, extended vacancy, remodeling, storm damage or a neighboring construction project. Do not decide that one event caused the problem; simply preserve the sequence.

Also identify access constraints before the appointment: locked gates, pets, tenants, attic hatches, crawl-space entries, stored items or rooms that require notice. The goal is not to prepare the house for a television reveal. It is to help the service team understand what can be inspected and which areas may need a separate plan.

- Moisture or leak history
- Landscape and mulch changes
- Deliveries, vacancy or remodeling
- Gates, pets and access limits

## What not to do before the inspection

Avoid blanket spraying, removing every visible sign, opening walls, handling wildlife, mixing products or moving heavy equipment into unsafe positions. Those actions can create exposure risks, erase useful context or turn a service question into a repair problem. If a label-directed safety action is necessary, follow the product label and tell the professional exactly what was applied, where and when.

Do not promise yourself that a clean photograph means the rest of the structure is clear. Florida’s official WDO form states that the report is based on visible and readily accessible areas and is not a structural-damage report. If a qualified inspector identifies a structural concern, a building-trade professional may be needed for a separate evaluation.

- Do not disturb structural material
- Do not mix or improvise treatments
- Keep product names and application times
- Treat a visual inspection and structural review as different scopes

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I photograph before a pest inspection?

Capture one wide view for room or exterior context, one medium view showing the location, and one close detail. Preserve timestamps and avoid filters. Photograph only visible, safely accessible areas; do not open walls, move energized equipment or disturb structural material for a better image.

### Should I clean up termite wings or pellets before the appointment?

If it is safe to leave them, photograph the material in place and preserve a small, undisturbed area for the inspector. Avoid deciding that the sign proves a termite species. FDACS and UF/IFAS both emphasize identification because Florida has different termite groups and treatment paths.

### Is a WDO inspection the same as general pest control?

No. Florida distinguishes general household pest work from termites and other wood-destroying organisms. A real-estate WDO report also uses a prescribed state form and scope. Ask the company which category and report apply to your property and purpose.

### Does a visual pest inspection prove there is no hidden damage?

No. Florida’s WDO report explains that inaccessible and concealed areas may not be covered and that the report is not a structural-damage report. Hidden conditions can require additional investigation by the appropriate qualified professional.
