What it's actually about

You're already carrying a lot. This lands on top of it.

A court date. Emails from a lawyer. A schedule nobody has figured out yet, and kids who need dinner regardless. Then a form tells you there's a class too, and it's hard to read that as anything but one more hoop.

So this is built to stay out of your way. Read a lesson in a waiting room. Close it, and the clock stops when you do. Come back tomorrow or next week and it's sitting where you left it.

The hours aren't filler, though. What predicts how kids do afterward isn't the divorce itself, it's how much of the fighting reaches them, and most of the course goes to the part you're probably dreading: dealing with the other parent, for years, without your children carrying it for you.

Questions first?
Before you buy

What people ask before they buy.

Will my court accept this certificate?

Ask your clerk of court first. ParentPath covers the topic areas Florida requires, but it is not currently an approved course provider, and each circuit keeps its own accepted-provider list. If your court requires a provider from that list, use one.

Can I get through it faster than four hours?

No. The timer runs only while a lesson is open, stops when you switch apps, and won't rack up credit while the screen sits idle. Four hours of credit means four hours of reading.

What if I fail a quiz?

Retake it as many times as you need. 80% passes a module.

Is any of this legal advice?

No, it's education. Statutes and local practice vary by circuit, so talk to a Florida attorney about your own case.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-in anywhere in the app.

ParentPath is an educational product, not an approved course provider. Court acceptance varies by jurisdiction.
