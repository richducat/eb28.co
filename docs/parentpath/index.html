The problem

The course is required. Most of them are a slideshow from 2009.

You didn't choose this class, and you're taking it during the worst month of your life. The research is blunt about what actually decides how your kids come out of this: not the divorce — the conflict around it. So a course worth four hours should teach you the thing that matters, which is how to talk to someone you're furious with, for the next fifteen years, without your children absorbing it. ParentPath is built around that, and it tracks your time honestly enough that the certificate means something.

How it works

A co-parent who pushes back

Five scenarios drawn from the fights that actually happen: the late pickup, the money jab, the new partner, the swap request, and the message written purely to wound. You reply. The conversation cools down when you get it right and escalates when you take the bait.

Questions first?
Before you buy

The honest answers.

Will my court accept this certificate?

Ask your clerk of court first. ParentPath covers the topic areas Florida requires, but it is not currently an approved course provider, and each circuit keeps its own accepted-provider list. If your court requires a provider from that list, use one.

Can I finish it faster than four hours?

No, and that's the point. The timer runs only while a lesson is open on screen, pauses when you switch apps, and caps idle bursts. Four hours of credit means four hours of reading.

What if I fail a quiz?

Retake it as many times as you need. 80% passes a module.

Is this legal advice?

No. It's education. Statutes and local practice vary by circuit — talk to a licensed Florida attorney about your own case.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-in anywhere in the app.

ParentPath is an educational product, not an approved course provider. Court acceptance varies by jurisdiction — confirm accepted providers with the clerk of court in your circuit before relying on this course to satisfy an order. It is not legal advice and is not a substitute for an attorney.
